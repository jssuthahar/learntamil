To teach a child Tamil at home, start with listening, then learn the 12 vowels, the 18 consonants and the uyirmei letters one row at a time, and read simple picture words — about 10 minutes every day. This guide gives the order to follow, a daily routine and free tools that help, even if you do not read Tamil yourself.

🪜Step by step

  1. Listen first. Let your child hear Tamil every day: talk, sing rhymes (நிலா நிலா ஓடி வா) and read short stories aloud.
  2. Learn the 12 vowels. Tap each vowel (உயிர்) to hear it, with its picture word. Learn the short and long pairs together: அ–ஆ, இ–ஈ, உ–ஊ.
  3. Learn the 18 consonants. Learn the consonants (மெய்) in their three groups: hard, soft and medium sounds.
  4. Build uyirmei letters. Join a consonant and a vowel: க் + ஆ = கா. Learn one row of the uyirmei chart at a time.
  5. Write with your finger, then a pencil. Trace letters on screen (Games → Trace), then on paper with the free tracing worksheets.
  6. Read words, then sentences. Read two-letter picture words letter by letter, then three-letter words, then sentences from the stories.
  7. Keep it short, daily and happy. Ten minutes a day beats an hour once a week. Celebrate every letter — the app gives tokens, badges, stickers and a daily goal.
  8. Check progress. Take the short level tests for a certificate, and look at the Parent dashboard to see the learning path and which letters need more practice.

⏱️A 10-minute daily routine

  • 2 minutes — hear it: the letter of the day on the app’s home screen, or one Aathichoodi line.
  • 3 minutes — play it: one game such as Listen & pick or Build a letter.
  • 3 minutes — write it: trace the letter on screen or on a worksheet.
  • 2 minutes — use it: find the letter in a word around the house — on a food packet, a calendar or a book.

👂Sounds that need extra practice

Some Tamil letters sound alike to young children: ந ன ண (three kinds of n), ல ள ழ (l, a curled l, and the special Tamil ழ) and ர ற (soft and strong r). Practise them in pairs and listen carefully — the Listen game never puts letters that sound alike side by side, so a child is not confused before they are ready.

🧒By age

  • Ages 3–5: listening, rhymes, pictures and the 12 vowels. Use Little ones mode — pictures only, no wrong-answer buzzer.
  • Ages 5–7: all 30 vowels and consonants, tracing, uyirmei rows and two-letter words.
  • Ages 7 and up: all 247 letters, spelling, reading sentences and the certificate levels.

❓Questions parents ask

At what age can a child start learning Tamil letters?

Most children can start hearing and naming Tamil letters at about 3 to 4 years old, and start writing them at about 4 to 5. Younger children learn best by listening, singing and pointing at pictures; reading and writing come later.

How long does it take to learn the 247 Tamil letters?

With 10 to 15 minutes a day, many children learn the 12 vowels and 18 consonants in a few weeks. The 216 uyirmei letters follow a pattern (a consonant plus a vowel sign), so once the vowel signs are known, a new row is quick to learn. Every child is different — short daily practice matters more than speed.

In what order should a child learn Tamil letters?

Start with the 12 vowels (உயிர்), then the 18 consonants (மெய்), then the uyirmei letters one consonant row at a time (க கா கி கீ …). Then read two-letter words such as ஆடு and மீன், then three-letter words, and then short stories.

Can I teach Tamil if I cannot read it myself?

Yes. Learn Tamil plays the sound of every letter and word and checks answers in the games, so a parent can learn alongside the child. Speaking Tamil at home every day helps most of all.

How can children living outside Tamil Nadu learn Tamil?

Use Tamil at home for everyday things — food, family names, colours, counting — and add 10 minutes of letters a day. Rhymes, stories and festival talk keep it fun. The app works offline after the first visit, and the printable worksheets give practice away from the screen.
